BOND CORE ARCHITECTURE

Five Factors

The structure behind BOND evaluation

BOND organizes Human–AI interaction evaluation into five core factors. Each factor contains five sub-factors, creating a 25-sub-factor structure for examining how an AI understands, communicates, adapts, remains reliable, and responds throughout interaction.

01

FACTOR 01

Understanding

How effectively the AI interprets requirements, intent, context, constraints, and changes in the user's request.

WHAT IT REPRESENTS

Understanding examines whether the system can identify what the user is actually asking for rather than responding only to isolated words. It covers the recognition of requirements, intended meaning, relevant context, explicit constraints, and later clarifications or updates.

FACTOR WEIGHT 20% Equal-weight BOND architecture
UR

Requirement Recognition

Identifying the explicit task, requested output, and requirements contained in the user's message.

Measures: recognition of what must be done.
UI

Intent Interpretation

Interpreting the intended meaning or objective behind the user's wording.

Measures: understanding of what the user means.
UC

Context Integration

Incorporating relevant information from the current interaction when it affects the response.

Measures: use of relevant interaction context.
UCR

Constraint Recognition

Detecting and respecting explicit limits such as format, length, scope, or requested conditions.

Measures: recognition of response constraints.
UU

Clarification & Update Handling

Incorporating clarifications, corrections, or updated requirements as the interaction progresses.

Measures: response to changed understanding.

02

FACTOR 02

Clarity

How clearly and effectively the AI communicates information so that the response can be understood and used.

WHAT IT REPRESENTS

Clarity focuses on the quality of communication itself: whether the response is understandable, organized, precise, focused on the relevant task, and sufficiently explanatory for the purpose of the interaction.

FACTOR WEIGHT 20% Equal-weight BOND architecture
CC

Comprehensibility

How easily the intended meaning of the response can be understood by the user.

Measures: ease of understanding.
CS

Structural Organization

How logically the response is arranged through ordering, grouping, and presentation.

Measures: organization and flow.
CP

Precision

How accurately and specifically the response communicates the information relevant to the task.

Measures: specificity and precision.
CR

Relevance & Focus

How closely the response remains focused on the user's actual request without unnecessary deviation.

Measures: relevance to the request.
CE

Explanatory Sufficiency

Whether the response provides enough explanation to make the answer useful for the intended purpose.

Measures: adequacy of explanation.
↑ Back to factor map
03

FACTOR 03

Personalization

How appropriately the AI adapts to relevant human context, user-level needs, preferences, goals, and situation.

WHAT IT REPRESENTS

Personalization examines selective adaptation rather than simply adding more personal information. The factor focuses on recognizing relevant context, adapting to the user's level and preferences, responding to goals and situations, and using context only when it is useful to the interaction.

FACTOR WEIGHT 20% Equal-weight BOND architecture
PCR

Relevant Context Recognition

Recognizing which available human or interaction context is relevant to the current task.

Measures: relevance of context recognition.
PLA

User-Level Adaptation

Adjusting explanation, complexity, or communication style to the apparent level of the user.

Measures: adaptation to user level.
PPA

Preference Adaptation

Respecting relevant preferences expressed during the interaction.

Measures: use of stated preferences.
PGS

Goal & Situation Adaptation

Adapting the response to the user's current goal and situation when those factors affect the task.

Measures: fit to goal and situation.
PSC

Selective Context Use

Using relevant context without unnecessarily inserting unrelated or distracting personal information.

Measures: appropriate and selective context use.
↑ Back to factor map
04

FACTOR 04

Reliability

How consistently the AI maintains appropriate interaction behavior across turns and handles uncertainty or errors.

WHAT IT REPRESENTS

Reliability concerns consistency over time. It examines whether interaction behavior remains stable, whether relevant context and constraints persist, whether uncertainty is communicated appropriately, and whether the system can recover from mistakes or failed turns.

FACTOR WEIGHT 20% Equal-weight BOND architecture
RBC

Behavioral Consistency

Maintaining appropriate response behavior rather than changing unpredictably across comparable interaction states.

Measures: consistency of interaction behavior.
RCC

Contextual Continuity

Maintaining continuity with relevant information already established in the interaction.

Measures: continuity across turns.
RCP

Constraint Persistence

Continuing to respect relevant constraints after they have been established.

Measures: persistence of requirements.
RUC

Uncertainty Calibration

Communicating uncertainty appropriately when the system lacks sufficient confidence or information.

Measures: appropriate handling of uncertainty.
RER

Error Recovery

Correcting or recovering from an identified mistake while continuing the interaction appropriately.

Measures: recovery after errors or corrections.
↑ Back to factor map
05

FACTOR 05

Engagement & Responsiveness

How effectively the AI recognizes interaction signals and adapts as the interaction evolves toward the user's goal.

WHAT IT REPRESENTS

Engagement & Responsiveness focuses on the dynamic side of interaction. It examines whether the AI notices signals from the user, responds to feedback, understands progression, continues adaptively, and keeps its responses oriented toward the user's goal.

FACTOR WEIGHT 20% Equal-weight BOND architecture
ERS

Interaction Signal Recognition

Recognizing meaningful signals contained in the user's wording, feedback, corrections, or interaction behavior.

Measures: recognition of interaction signals.
ERF

Feedback Responsiveness

Adjusting the response appropriately when the user provides feedback about what is or is not working.

Measures: response to feedback.
ERP

Progression Awareness

Understanding where the interaction currently stands and what has already been completed or established.

Measures: awareness of interaction progression.
ERA

Adaptive Continuation

Continuing the interaction in a way that incorporates previous turns and newly relevant information.

Measures: adaptation across continued interaction.
ERG

Goal-Oriented Engagement

Keeping interaction behavior aligned with the user's underlying objective rather than engagement for its own sake.

Measures: alignment with the user's goal.

BOND CALCULATION

From 25 sub-factors to one framework score

The current BOND architecture uses equal weighting at each level. Each factor is the average of its five sub-factor scores, and the overall BOND score is the average of the five factors.

Understanding

U = (UR + UI + UC + UCR + UU) / 5

Five Understanding sub-factors contribute equally to the Understanding factor.

Clarity

C = (CC + CS + CP + CR + CE) / 5

Five Clarity sub-factors contribute equally to the Clarity factor.

Personalization

P = (PCR + PLA + PPA + PGS + PSC) / 5

Five Personalization sub-factors contribute equally to the Personalization factor.

Reliability

R = (RBC + RCC + RCP + RUC + RER) / 5

Five Reliability sub-factors contribute equally to the Reliability factor.

Engagement & Responsiveness

ER = (ERS + ERF + ERP + ERA + ERG) / 5

Five Engagement & Responsiveness sub-factors contribute equally to the factor.

Overall BOND

BOND = (U + C + P + R + ER) / 5

The five core factors are equally weighted in the current framework.

Interpretation: The numerical scoring scale and empirical validation of these measurements are part of the experimental BOND research prototype. This page defines the framework architecture and calculation structure; it does not claim that the framework has already been scientifically validated.
