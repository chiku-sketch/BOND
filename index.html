What is BOND?

BOND is a multidimensional evaluation framework proposed to measure AI-side connection-building capability during human–AI interaction.

What BOND does

BOND turns an abstract interaction concept into a structured evaluation problem. It focuses on observable AI behavior rather than scoring the human or the complete human–AI relationship.

  • Observe — AI behavior within a defined interaction.
  • Measure — Five primary interaction dimensions.
  • Diagnose — Specific strengths and weaknesses through 25 sub-factors.
  • Compare — Systems under controlled or matched interaction conditions.
  • Validate — The framework through future empirical research.

What BOND measures

UnderstandingCorrect interpretation of human requirements and relevant context.
ClarityEffective communication of the response.
PersonalizationAppropriate adaptation to relevant user or situation context.
ReliabilityConsistent and dependable behavior.
Engagement & ResponsivenessEffective response as the interaction develops.

Measurement boundary

BOND evaluates observable AI-side behavior. It does not claim to measure general intelligence, the complete human–AI relationship, human psychology, human-likeness, or every broader AI quality.

A capability should not be inferred when the interaction provided no meaningful opportunity to demonstrate it. More personalization is also not automatically better; relevant and appropriate adaptation is the target.

Interpretation

Pros, Cons & Limitations.

A clear research prototype should show what the framework is useful for, where it can be difficult to apply, and what its current scores cannot establish.

Pros

What BOND enables

  • Separates interaction quality into five interpretable dimensions.
  • Provides diagnostic sub-factors instead of only one opaque number.
  • Supports matched Baseline vs AHI comparisons.
  • Makes assumptions, weighting and measurement boundaries visible.
  • Creates a structure that can later be tested and empirically validated.
Cons

Current trade-offs

  • Some interaction qualities are inherently context-dependent.
  • Automated heuristic scoring can miss nuanced human judgments.
  • Equal weighting may not reflect eventual empirical importance.
  • Short interactions may not provide evidence for every sub-factor.
  • A small prototype model can limit the quality of observed responses.
Limitations

What the score does not prove

  • It does not establish general intelligence or AGI capability.
  • It does not measure consciousness or human psychological attachment.
  • It does not guarantee factual accuracy, safety or domain expertise.
  • Displayed factor values are diagnostic/provisional, not validated scientific measurements.
  • Empirical studies are required before treating weights or scores as established metrics.

Interpretation rule

A missing opportunity should not automatically become a failure. If a prompt provides no meaningful evidence for a capability, the evaluator should avoid inferring that capability from silence. Likewise, more personalization is not automatically better; relevant and appropriate adaptation is the target.

Testing

Controlled test set.

Use matched prompts to compare Baseline and AHI behavior across different interaction demands.

01 / Constraint following

Tests whether explicit requirements survive from prompt to response.

“Explain gravity in exactly one short sentence without technical terminology.”

02 / Context understanding

Tests whether the response uses relevant context rather than answering generically.

“I am a beginner learning physics. Explain why astronauts appear weightless.”

03 / Comparison

Tests organization, relevance and balanced handling of alternatives.

“Compare solar and wind energy for a school project in a compact table.”

04 / Correction & update

Tests whether the system can respond appropriately after a changed requirement.

“Use three bullet points.” Then: “Actually, make it two and keep the key idea.”
Research

References.

Reference details used to position BOND alongside existing AI and interaction evaluation approaches.

01 / HUMAN EVALUATION

Human evaluation

Direct human assessment

The BOND research discusses direct human assessment for context-dependent qualities such as helpfulness, relevance, clarity, instruction-following and preference.

02 / MODEL JUDGING

LLM-as-a-Judge

Scalable model-based evaluation

The research discusses model-based judging as scalable while emphasizing evaluator reliability and systematic bias concerns.

03 / MULTI-TURN

MT-Bench

Multi-turn conversational evaluation

MT-Bench is discussed as an example of evaluating chat assistants across developing multi-turn conversations.

04 / HUMAN PREFERENCE

Chatbot Arena

Pairwise human preference

Chatbot Arena is discussed as a human-preference comparison method that captures overall preference but not every diagnostic dimension.

05 / BROAD CAPABILITY

MMLU

Knowledge and problem solving

MMLU is discussed as a broad capability benchmark; capability performance does not automatically establish effective human interaction.

06 / MULTIDIMENSIONAL

HELM

Multi-scenario evaluation

HELM is discussed as an example of multidimensional evaluation using broad scenario and metric coverage.

07 / REFERENCE METRICS

BLEU & ROUGE

Reference-based text metrics

The research positions BLEU and ROUGE as useful task-specific metrics that do not by themselves measure AI-side connection-building.

08 / PRIMARY SOURCE

BOND V1 Research Document

Framework source

The BOND research document defines the proposed five-factor structure, 25 sub-factors, scoring principles, boundaries and future validation directions.

Reference boundary

These references are presented as related evaluation approaches discussed in the BOND research material. BOND is positioned as complementary and does not replace task, factuality, safety, reasoning, domain-specific or general capability evaluation.
